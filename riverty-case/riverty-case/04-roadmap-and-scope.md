Riverty Consumer Card: MVP Scope, Roadmap and Merchant Fit

Planning assumptions: The brief doesn’t define the card’s credit mechanics or the regulatory path, so I flag both as decisions for Risk, Compliance and Finance. Nothing below is a committed timeline. Phases are gated by evidence, not dates.

1. MVP Scope (Friends & Family, Germany)

Design principle: every feature must either test the core hypothesis (does Lena use the card repeatedly, off-partner, without hurting partner volume?) or be required for safe operation. Everything else waits. Because each new proposition needs its own approval covering proposition, pricing, credit policy and terms, a smaller proposition surface is the fastest route through approval.

MUST-HAVES

Area	Scope	Why
Card and rails	Virtual card first, usable online and in-store via phone wallet, on the existing Mastercard and Paymentology platform	Tests the coverage gap directly and reuses what the Amazon Business pilot is already paying for, rather than creating new platform work
Credit mechanic	One simple product: fixed limit with automatic repayment, no revolving interest in the MVP	This is the “standard credit card” idea already named in the brief, and the simplest to explain and approve. It tests behaviour, not interest income. Confirm with Regulatory whether it needs full approval or can run as a limited F&F pilot.
Control and transparency	Visible limit, upcoming repayment date and amount, clear terms, instant freeze	Directly serves Lena’s need for control and supports approval
Onboarding and risk	In-app application for existing Riverty users, using existing credit policy and data where permitted	Existing users are the warm entry point, and re-using data avoids new risk build
Basic servicing	Transaction list, support path, dispute handling, fraud monitoring	Required for safe operation, even at small scale
Measurement	Event tracking for the three KPIs, including partner-checkout volume for cardholders vs. a control group	Without this the test can’t produce a decision

DELIBERATELY LEFT OUT

Revolving credit and interest income (Later). It adds regulatory and credit-loss complexity, and the MVP is about behaviour. Trade-off: this defers testing the part of the business case that relies on interest.
Physical plastic card (Next), unless F&F feedback shows a virtual card blocks real usage
Rewards and cashback. Entering an incentive race with Klarna and PayPal costs margin and doesn’t test our control hypothesis.
Apple Pay-Later wallet slot, which the brief says is currently blocked by CCD2 and its written-form requirement
Segment cards: creator, public transport, B2B variants. Amazon Business is already the priority workstream.
Other markets and currencies
Public marketing and brand campaigns
Complex features (budgeting tools, multiple limits, split-bill, etc.)
2. Three-Phase Roadmap
Phase	Scope	Exit gate to next phase
NOW: Friends & Family MVP (Germany)	The must-haves above, with a small group of existing users and employees. Goal: learn, not scale.	Activation and repeat use meet agreed thresholds. No decline in partner checkout volume. Arrears and fraud are within risk appetite. Regulatory path confirmed for broader release.
NEXT: Public launch in Germany	Offer to eligible existing Riverty users, with physical card if justified. Targeted in-checkout and in-app promotion. Merchant-facing benefits (see section 3). Re-test conversion against the 5% assumption.	Real conversion and spend per user support the business case. Unit economics are positive after credit losses. Operations and risk scale without displacing committed programmes.
LATER: European expansion and advanced credit	Revolving credit and interest, additional markets (the brief mentions DACH, Benelux, Nordics), and a later review of options such as wallet slots as constraints ease. Each needs its own regulatory approval.	Decided on evidence from the earlier phases.

Sequencing note: Priorities against Amazon Business, creator and wallet ideas should be set with explicit criteria (strategic fit, evidence, regulatory effort, capacity needed) rather than by whichever workstream pushes hardest, which is how the brief says it has worked so far.

3. How the MVP Strengthens the Merchant-First Strategy

Design stance: the card extends Riverty’s reach without replacing the checkout relationship. These are hypotheses to prove, not claims.

Complements, doesn’t bypass. At partner merchants, Lena should keep being offered Riverty checkout. The card is for where Riverty can’t be. This keeps merchant economics and conversion intact.
Fills the coverage gap without taking volume. Off-partner and in-store spend is new reach rather than diverted partner volume. The incrementality KPI checks this directly. If cardholders reduce partner checkout, we stop and rethink.
A path to benefits for merchants. In the Next phase, we can explore giving partner merchants something concrete, such as a loyal customer who is more likely to choose Riverty, or in-app discovery of partner merchants. Whether that actually lifts conversion is untested.
Reinforces Riverty’s brand with users. Stronger brand preference (about 6% today) should raise the chance Riverty is picked when a checkout offers a choice, which benefits merchants who offer it.
Doesn’t compete for scarce capacity. Using the platform the Amazon Business pilot already funds, and keeping the MVP small, means the card supports the strategy rather than draining it.

Risks to watch: cannibalisation of merchant-paid BNPL fees if card spend replaces checkout spend, and merchant perception that a Riverty card promotes competing merchants. Enterprise merchants underpin profitability, so I’d brief a few of them early, before public launch.
