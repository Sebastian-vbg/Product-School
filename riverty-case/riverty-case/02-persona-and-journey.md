Lena’s Workarounds and Why Riverty Loses Her

What the brief says vs. what I’m inferring. The brief gives us Lena’s behaviour but doesn’t document her in-store workarounds or how she moves between tools. I’ve separated what’s stated from what’s a hypothesis to validate in discovery.

1. What the brief actually tells us
She shops almost entirely on her phone and uses BNPL on most non-trivial purchases.
She prefers money to leave her account only after she has decided to keep the item. The brief says this isn’t about affordability.
She has Klarna and PayPal installed and uses whichever option appears at checkout.
She knows Riverty but doesn’t prefer it and rarely goes looking for it.
Riverty has no in-store presence, and loyal users can only use it at partner merchants.
2. Her workarounds

Online (well supported by the brief)

Situation	Workaround	Tool
Merchant offers Riverty	Uses it, by default or by chance, not by preference	Riverty
Merchant offers Klarna or PayPal instead	Takes the option shown, with no loyalty to any provider	Klarna, PayPal
Merchant offers none of the BNPL options she knows	Falls back to a conventional payment (hypothesis)	Existing debit or credit card

The core workaround is behavioural: she outsources the provider choice to the merchant’s checkout. She doesn’t compare, switch or seek out Riverty. The brief describes this as her actual behaviour.

In physical stores (hypotheses only)

The brief confirms Riverty isn’t available in-store but doesn’t say what Lena does there. Plausible workarounds, all unvalidated:

Pays with a debit card, so money leaves her account immediately and she loses the “decide first” control.
Uses PayPal’s or Klarna’s card or in-store features if she has them (the brief lists PayPal’s cards and the Klarna Card as alternatives, but not whether Lena holds them).
Postpones or moves the purchase online to get BNPL (my guess, worth asking in interviews).
3. Why these workarounds make Riverty lose her

The brief supports these mechanisms directly:

No ownership of the moment of choice. Because she takes whatever appears, Riverty only wins when a merchant happens to show it. That is the 6% brand preference problem.
No presence between checkouts. Riverty has no daily touchpoint with her. Klarna and PayPal are installed apps she opens independently, and Riverty’s relationship exists only at and after checkout.
Coverage gap. Outside partner merchants and in stores, Riverty simply can’t serve her, so those purchases go to competitors by default.
Heavy users are the most exposed. The brief calls loyal, frequent users the most under-monetised, so each lost purchase hits our best customers hardest.

Inferred consequence (not stated in the brief): if Lena’s need for timing control is met elsewhere in stores, she never builds a Riverty habit there, and the card’s spend ramp assumption (EUR 4,000 to 7,000) depends on exactly that habit forming.

4. What to validate in discovery
How does Lena actually pay in stores, and what does she do when she’d have used BNPL online?
Does she hold a Klarna or PayPal card, or only the apps?
When she picks a provider at checkout, is it truly indifferent, or does she have unstated preferences (past experience, limits, fees)?
Would “decide first, pay later” in a card get her to choose Riverty, or only to add another option she uses occasionally?
