Launch Recommendation: Riverty Consumer Card (Germany)

Headline: Proceed with a small, gated Friends & Family test now. Don’t commit to a public launch until the test shows that Lena uses the card repeatedly, off-partner, without reducing partner-merchant volume. All thresholds below are illustrative and need to be set with Finance, Risk and Regulatory. The brief gives no approved targets.

1. Go-To-Market: first active cardholders without hurting merchants

Principle: acquire from users Riverty already has, in channels Riverty controls, and never in a way that nudges them away from partner checkouts.

Step	Approach	Merchant safeguard
Start with F&F	Invite employees and a small group of friends and family who are existing Riverty users	No merchant exposure at all during the first test
Then heavy users	Invite the most frequent existing users (the “underserved heavy users” gap) through Riverty’s own app and post-purchase communication	Offers appear in Riverty-owned channels, not on merchant checkout pages, so no merchant real estate is used
Position the card by where it works	Message: “decide first, pay later, also where Riverty checkout isn’t offered”	Wording never suggests choosing the card over a Riverty checkout at partner merchants
Brief key merchants early	Share the purpose and guardrails with a few enterprise and SME partners before any public launch	Builds trust and surfaces objections early
Later: merchant upside	Explore merchant-facing benefits (for example, loyal customers returning to partners) only once evidence shows they exist	Avoids promising benefits we haven’t proven

What we avoid: paid campaigns that compete with merchants for their customers, promoting non-partner merchants inside partner checkouts, and any incentive that rewards using the card where Riverty checkout is already available.

2. Go / No-Go Decision Criteria

These signals map to the KPIs defined earlier (activation, repeat use, merchant incrementality) plus risk guardrails. Set thresholds in advance, so we aren’t tempted to rationalise results afterwards.

Decision	Signals
SCALE (move to public launch in Germany)	Activation approaches the modelled 70%. A meaningful share of activated users make repeat off-partner or in-store purchases. No decline in partner-merchant checkout volume vs. control group. Arrears and fraud within risk appetite. Regulatory path for broader release confirmed. Unit economics are plausible after credit losses.
PIVOT (change the proposition)	Activation is healthy but repeat use is low, suggesting the card gets added but not used, so we revisit the value proposition (is control really the driver?). Or use is concentrated at partner merchants rather than off-partner, meaning we’re not filling the gap. Or users want something else (for example, a different repayment structure).
STOP or PAUSE	Partner-merchant checkout volume falls among cardholders (cannibalisation, which conflicts with merchant-first). Or activation is far below the benchmark with no clear fix. Or arrears and fraud exceed risk appetite. Or regulatory requirements make the proposition unviable in its current form.

Caveat: F&F users are not representative of the wider market, so a successful test supports “continue”, not “the 5% conversion is proven”. Public launch must re-test conversion and spend per user against the business case. Before scaling, also check that the case works on revenue, not just volume, since interchange may be small and the interest pool is contested by instalment card banks.

3. Executive Pitch Summary

Riverty’s merchant-first model has built a strong BNPL base across DACH, Benelux and the Nordics, but it leaves three structural gaps: we are absent from physical stores, our most loyal heavy users can only use us where partner merchants offer it, and brand preference at checkout sits at around 6%. Customers like Lena, who uses BNPL on most non-trivial purchases and takes whichever provider a merchant’s checkout shows her, give us a strong clue: she values deciding first and paying later, but today that control lives with the merchant, not with her. A Riverty consumer card carries that behaviour into stores and non-partner shops, gives heavy users a reason to build a habit with us, and creates a direct, everyday relationship that can lift brand preference, while adding interchange and, over time, interest revenue. We recommend a deliberately small Friends & Family launch on the platform already funded by the Amazon Business pilot, with strict guardrails: the card complements partner checkouts rather than replacing them, and we scale only if cardholders use it repeatedly and partner-merchant volume holds. That discipline makes it a move that extends Riverty’s reach without compromising the merchants who underpin our profitability and growth.

Open items for leadership: confirm the regulatory approval path for the MVP, validate the business case on revenue (not just volume), and agree the ranking criteria against Amazon Business and the other competing initiatives so capacity is allocated deliberately.
