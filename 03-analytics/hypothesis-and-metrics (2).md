## Step 3 · Craft your hypothesis
- **Qualitative evidence (from M2) , quote the specific friction / moment of misery for your persona:** Lena relies on BNPL but lacks in-store BNPL options and faces fragmented payment methods across merchants, resulting in low brand preference (6%).
- **Quantitative evidence (from M3) , name the metric or data point that confirms the pain; cite the number:** Modelling assumes 5% user conversion to card, 70% activation, 5% annual churn, and spend rising from EUR 4,000 (Year 1) to EUR 7,000 (Year 3) per active user.
- **Persona , role, goal, and the friction you confirmed in the reconciliation steps:** Lena, 32, in Cologne: a mobile-first shopper on a middling income seeking seamless payment flexibility and financial control both online and in-store.
- **Problem you are solving , one sentence describing the specific friction this initiative removes:** Eliminates the friction of forced payment switching between online checkouts and physical stores by providing a single, everywhere-accepted consumer card.
- **Strategic outcome , what behaviour change do you expect, and how does it map to retention / revenue / churn?:** Drives everyday card usage outside partner merchants, capturing in-store transactions to increase spend per user to EUR 7,000 and boost core brand preference.
- **Primary success metric (initiative signal) , the leading indicator that tells you the gap is closing:** Card activation rate (target 70%) and average active card spend (target EUR 4,000 in Year 1).
- **Guardrail metric (product signal) , the metric that must NOT drop; it protects your existing base:** Annual cardholder churn rate (must remain below 5%)
- **Decision window , how much time or data before you scale, pivot, or kill? minimum threshold to proceed?:** 6 months post-launch of the Friends & Family MVP in Germany (minimum 1,000 active pilot cardholders).
- **Draft your full hypothesis sentence , one to three sentences; quote the metric, name the persona, name the outcome:** Based on the brief’s observations that Lena, 32, a mobile-first BNPL shopper in Cologne, wants money to leave her account only after she decides to keep an item but cannot use Riverty at non-partner merchants or in stores, we believe that a Riverty card carrying “decide first, pay later” to those places will result in repeated everyday use instead of her debit card, Klarna or PayPal. We will measure this as at least 40% of activated cardholders making 2 or more off-partner or in-store purchases within 60 days, with monthly spend per active user trending toward EUR 333, while partner-merchant checkout volume does not decline versus a matched control group and arrears and fraud stay within risk appetite. We will make a go/pivot/no-go decision after a 6-month pilot.
