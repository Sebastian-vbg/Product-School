# GTM Launch Plan, Riverty business-unit case

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Feature | “What I Owe” Overview: one screen with total owed, itemised card purchases, and next repayment date and amount, for Riverty Card cardholders in the Germany Friends & Family pilot |
| Goal | Engagement |
| Launch tier | S, Minimal |

## Goal & Audience
- **Goal:** Engagement, Cardholders already hold the card, and R3 exists to help them understand what they owe and when, which protects the churn guardrail and supports repeat use. R3’s own rationale says it doesn’t drive activation by itself, so Conversion would misread it. The priority is that cardholders find and use the screen and come away knowing their balance. There is no public audience to make aware, since the card is in a closed pilot.

Audience: Pilot cardholders with at least one settled purchase on the Riverty Card. Lena-type users (32, Cologne, salaried, mobile-first, middling income, BNPL for non-trivial purchases) are the design target, but pilot membership doesn’t guarantee a match, so profile data will be compared against her after the pilot. During the A/B test, only the variant half sees the feature. Employees are analysed separately.
- **Target audience:** Pilot cardholders with at least one settled purchase on the Riverty Card. Lena-type users (32, Cologne, salaried, mobile-first, middling income, no financial cushion, BNPL for non-trivial purchases) are the design target, but pilot membership doesn’t guarantee a match, so pilot age and spending profile will be compared with Lena after the pilot. Users are assigned to an arm at first settled purchase, and during the A/B test only the variant half sees the feature. Cardholders with no purchase yet are not in the test, and the empty state only appears after rollout. Employees are analysed separately.

## Launch Tier
- **S, Minimal**, The pilot is about 1,000 active cardholders, all reached through Riverty’s own app, so reach is small and there is no public audience. Revenue impact is indirect: R3 protects retention and repeat use but doesn’t drive activation. The risk of silence is low, because the entry point sits on the home screen, but discovery is the whole plan, so the screen includes a short first-open explanation (part of the variant, not marketing). Marketing during the test would contaminate the A/B experiment, which requires that no message goes live for either arm. Moving to M (one in-app message and email to all pilot cardholders) requires every criterion in the decision table to hold.

## Channels
1. **Owned: In-app home-screen entry point to “What I Owe”, shown to variant users only. This is the discovery route in the test, and it reaches cardholders at the moment they open the app.**
2. **Owned: Post-test announcement to all pilot cardholders, in-app message first and email second. Not used during the test, so control users don’t see it. Email requires marketing consent under German and EU rules, to be confirmed with Compliance before the email is built.**
3. **Owned: None for the pilot**

## Enablement & Assets
German screen copy with legal and compliance review; in-app entry-point design and a short first-open explanation inside the screen; feature flag keyed to arm assignment, so control never sees the entry point, with a same-day kill switch; daily automated balance reconciliation against the card platform, alerting the Backend lead and Risk; day-14 survey (German, identical in both arms, three questions: amount owed, next repayment date, and the control statement; shown as a blocking prompt before the home screen loads, built by the Frontend lead); one-page awareness note for Customer Support before the test (the screen exists, escalate balance discrepancies to Risk, no change to routes or scripts); short post-test release note and in-app message; email only if Compliance confirms consent; support FAQ on balances and repayment and an agent briefing delivered after the test; analytics dashboard for the primary metric, control score, spend, repayment harm and survey response rate by arm; QA test plan covering balance accuracy against the card platform and the loading, empty and error states; a Risk and Finance sign-off note defining "owed" as settled purchases only; a decision table (ship, iterate, stop, inconclusive) signed by PM, Risk and Analytics before launch; a one-line note to partner success so enterprise merchants know checkout is unchanged.

## Ownership, Budget & Timeline
- **Ownership & budget:** PM owns the plan, experiment design, post-launch metrics and declaring the gate once R2, R3 and R4 work together. Frontend lead owns the screen, entry point, feature flag and the day-14 survey prompt. Backend lead owns the balance endpoint, its accuracy against Paymentology, the daily reconciliation and the kill switch. QA lead owns the test plan and release sign-off. Analytics owns arm assignment, survey scoring, response-rate monitoring, the dashboard and Phase 3 monitoring. UX copy writer owns the German wording of the screen and survey. Legal owns copy review. Risk and Compliance own the "owed" definition, control-arm approval, stop rules and email consent, and, with Customer Support, any customer communication after an incident. R4 product owner owns delivering the repayment calendar by the gate date, with each user's first repayment falling within 30 days of first settled purchase. Customer Support owns the agent awareness note before the test and the FAQ and briefing after. Partner success owns the merchant note. Budget: no paid media. Extra costs, each quoted by its owner before Phase 1 ends, with amounts added as a condition of sign-off: survey tooling (Analytics), German copy and legal review (Legal), and QA capacity, since the 2 QA engineers are shared with R2 and R4 (QA lead).
- **Timeline:** Weekly native releases on Kanban. Week 0 is the gate date, declared by the PM when R2, R3 and R4 all work together. If the gate moves, every date below moves with it. The weeks are illustrative until the gate date is confirmed.

Phase 1, before the gate (runs in parallel with R2 and R4): R4 product owner delivers the repayment calendar; Risk and Compliance confirm approval of the control arm and the "owed" definition; Analytics confirms the real weekly enrolment rate; Customer Support receives the one-page awareness note; build behind the feature flag with a same-day kill switch and daily balance reconciliation; QA balance accuracy against the card platform; internal release only to the build and QA team, who are excluded from the test population (pilot employees are included and analysed separately).

Phase 2, weeks 1-16 after the gate: randomise at first settled purchase; run the day-14 survey (14 days after first settled purchase, closing at day 21) in both arms; weekly guardrail review plus the daily reconciliation alert; no other change to the card journey. The target is 652 enrolled users (first settled purchase), about 13 weeks at 50 per week, which is an assumption to confirm with Analytics. The baseline is estimated from early control-arm data, blind to the effect. Week 4, operational check only: enrolment rate, arm split within 50/50 plus or minus 5 points, survey delivery working, no incidents. It can trigger a safety stop, never a declaration of success. Week 6: survey response rate by arm. Week 8: first descriptive look at knowledge and control scores, for safety and diagnosis only. Fallback: if cumulative enrolled users at the end of week 4 are below about 200, or the response rate is below 50% in either arm at week 6 (at a lower rate, 652 users no longer yield 163 responders per arm), Analytics and the PM decide whether to extend or move to a staged rollout with the same survey and guardrails.

Phase 3, weeks 18-21 plus 4 weeks of monitoring (Analytics): the guardrail window is 30 days after first settled purchase, so the last enrolled user finishes about week 17. Apply the decision table, agreed and signed before launch. Ship if: users correctly stating both amount and date are at least 15 points higher than control, p < 0.05; the control score is higher in variant, p < 0.05; spend per active user is no more than 10% below control; no wrong-balance incident; no repayment harm; arm split within 50/50 plus or minus 5 points and response rates within 10 points of each other. Iterate if the knowledge score improves but the control score or spend is inconclusive. Stop if there is any wrong-balance incident, 2 or more repayment-harm cases, or a Risk objection. Inconclusive if the confidence interval spans zero and 15 points: extend or move to a staged rollout. The PM decides, with Risk sign-off on stop rules. If ship, roll out to all pilot cardholders, send the announcement and brief Support. If iterate, fix and re-run. If stop, switch off the flag.

## Success Metrics
- **Metrics:** Share of cardholders with a settled purchase who correctly state both amount owed and next repayment date at day 14: at least +15 points vs control, p < 0.05.
Self-reported control score (“I always know what I owe and when it is due”, 4 or 5 out of 5): higher in variant, p < 0.05.
Diagnostic only: share of variant cardholders opening “What I Owe” in the 7 days before their repayment date. Target X is set before launch from internal-tester usage, or left as a reported figure with no target.

Guardrails: monthly spend per active user no more than 10% below control; no wrong-balance incident; no variant users missing a repayment because of a displayed figure.
- **Bad signal to watch for:** Any wrong balance, found by the daily reconciliation or reported: switch the flag off the same day (Backend lead), then Risk review. Two or more variant users missing a repayment because of a displayed figure: stop and review (Risk). Spend more than 10% below control on a 4-week rolling basis: escalate for review, since it is noisy at this size (PM). Entry-point taps below X% at week 4, with X set before launch from internal-tester usage: log it and plan a placement or copy change for the iterate cycle, not mid-test (Frontend lead). Three or more balance-related support contacts from variant cardholders in any 4 weeks, or a week-8 look showing no knowledge difference despite high opens: review copy and layout for the next cycle (PM and Customer Support). Survey response rate below 50% in either arm at week 6: apply the fallback (Analytics).
- **Likely post-launch decision:** Iterate. Trigger: the knowledge score rises but spend or the control score is inconclusive. Ship only if every criterion in the decision table holds. Stop on any wrong-balance incident or repayment harm.
