# Riverty Card (Germany): Problem Hook and Value Proposition

*Lead PM draft. Lena's motivations are hypotheses to validate in discovery. The case study says to find out, not assume.*

## 1. Problem Hook

**Lena doesn't choose a BNPL provider. She takes whichever one the checkout shows her.**

She has Klarna and PayPal installed and likes to pay only after she's decided to keep an item. That is a real need, but today it's met by the merchant's checkout, not by anything she owns. Riverty is invisible in the moment of choice (about 6% brand preference), absent in stores, and unreachable for loyal users outside partner merchants.

> *"Lena wants to decide first and pay second, everywhere she shops. Today that only works where a checkout happens to offer it, and the provider she gets is chosen by the merchant, not by her."*

**Why this hook works for Riverty:**
- It starts from Lena's observed behaviour (pay only after deciding to keep), not from our wish for a card.
- It names Riverty's strategic problem (no relationship, no brand pull) as the other side of Lena's problem (no control over where "pay later" works).
- It doesn't claim Klarna or PayPal fail her. They serve her well wherever they appear. The gap is that nothing she owns carries that control with her.

## 2. Value Proposition

**For Lena:**
> *"The Riverty Card puts 'decide first, pay later' in your wallet, so you choose when and where to use it, instead of waiting for a checkout to offer it."*

**Supporting pillars (to be validated, not assumed):**

1. **Control over timing.** Her money leaves the account only after she has decided to keep the purchase, with transparent repayment and clear limits. Control and transparency, not a pitch for more credit, is the emotional core for a salaried user with no cushion.
2. **One familiar logic, wider reach.** Riverty behaviour she already knows, usable beyond partner merchants, including in-store and on non-partner sites.
3. **A simple, honest product.** No gimmicks. Clear terms and fixed limits that help her stay in control, which is also what regulators will want to approve.

**For Riverty (internal value proposition):**
> *"A direct, everyday relationship with our most loyal users, with interchange on every transaction and interest on revolving balances, without taking volume or margin away from our merchants."*

## 3. The Balancing Act

| Tension | Our stance |
|---|---|
| **Klarna and PayPal are already on her phone** | Don't copy Klarna's Card. Klarna holds its own banking licence and has years of head start. We win on a narrower promise (control over timing for heavy BNPL users), not breadth. Lena's realistic first move is to add the card, not switch, so the card must earn a place next to what she has. |
| **Merchant-first strategy** | The card must send Lena toward Riverty merchants, not away from them. The principle: at partner merchants, the card complements Riverty checkout and doesn't replace it or bypass merchant economics. Outside the partner network it fills the gap. Open question: how to make it a conversion and loyalty asset for merchants. |
| **Competing for platform capacity** | The Amazon Business pilot funds the platform and sets priorities. The consumer card should be a lean MVP (friends-and-family scope) that reuses the same rails and shows evidence early. A Riverty-branded standard credit card also needs its own regulatory approval, which adds months, so scope discipline is a strategic choice here, not just a constraint. |

## 4. Which Gap Comes First

Of the three gaps, I'd lead with **underserved heavy users**. They're the most under-monetised, they already know Riverty, and they're the likeliest to show habit formation (the business case assumes spend rises from EUR 4,000 to EUR 7,000 per user). **In-store acceptance** follows naturally from being a card, so it's a feature, not the lead. **Brand pull** is an outcome we measure, not a problem we design for directly.

## 5. What We Deliberately Leave Out (MVP)

- Rewards and cashback arms race against Klarna and PayPal
- Apple Pay-Later wallet slot (blocked by CCD2 and its written-form requirement)
- Creator, transit and other segment cards
- Broad credit expansion beyond what regulatory approval supports

## 6. What I'd Test First

- **Does the "decide first, pay later" insight hold?** Interview and prototype-test Lena-like heavy users to see if timing control, not price or rewards, actually drives adding a card.
- **The 5% conversion assumption.** The business case is most fragile here. A cheap test is a waitlist or fake-door within Riverty's existing user base.
- **Merchant effect.** Does card use increase or cannibalise checkout volume at partner merchants? This decides whether the strategy holds.
- **Change-course trigger.** For example, if activation or repeat use stays well below the modelled 70% and the spend ramp, we narrow or pause before scaling.
